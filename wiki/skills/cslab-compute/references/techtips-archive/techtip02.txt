Greetings,

As mentioned in the summer, we are assuming that you have a laptop
to use as your computing console most of the time (from which you can
connect to an assortment of powerful servers).

Many people have requested a computer monitor, keyboard, and/or mouse to
use with their laptop.  You can make a request or check that a request is
pending at https://mscac.cs.toronto.edu/accessories

If there are any of these items (monitor/keyboard/mouse/video-cables) at
your desk which you no longer want, I can remove them -- please just send
e-mail to ask.  Or any additional requests or changes can be sent by e-mail.

These items do have to stay in the room unless you have permission to
use them elsewhere.  In any case they need to be left at your desk when
you leave for your internships at the end of April.


There are two printers in the north-west end of the space.  Instructions
for configuring your laptop to print on these printers can be found at
https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~pocpm/printing.html
or you're welcome to ask me for assistance with this.

We will be supplying paper for the printers, but we're hoping that
you can do the actual replacing of the paper yourselves when needed.
The paper trays each only hold one ream of paper, so easiest is to let
it run out completely before adding more paper.  There is paper in the
deskside cabinets below the printers.

There are also two desktop computers by the printers, meant for quick use
to print something, quickly check your e-mail, etc, e.g. if you don't
bring your laptop some day.  All of you automatically have accounts on
these two computers, with a copy of your CS password, even though they're
not run by the CS core computing people (they are run by me).

The most important consequence of the fact that they are not actually CSLab
machines is that your home directory on these machines is local.  It's also
not backed up, so don't store the only copy of anything important there.

Please leave these computers turned on so that I can manage them remotely;
and please don't reboot them unnecessarily, or try to perform administrative
operations.  If you need certain software installed, please ask.  If you
can't manage to get an application to quit, you can either log in on
a text console or log in via ssh to kill processes as needed (their
names are gyre.red.sandbox and gimble.red.sandbox, as labelled).  Or,
ctrl+alt+backspace should usually log you out.  Or if you really must
reboot the machine, please do a proper shutdown:  You can press ctrl+alt+F3
to switch to a text console, then you can press ctrl+alt+delete to do a
proper shutdown and reboot.


Any questions about the computer equipment, workstations, or anything else,
please ask.
