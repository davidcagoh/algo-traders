# Cross-Cutting Learnings

Facts, hypotheses, and ruled-out directions that apply to **both** subprojects. Subproject-specific learnings stay in their own wikis.

---

## Confirmed (cross-project)

- **LLM-driven sequential strategy refinement gets geometrically trapped.** Both feishu and backtesting independently saw an LLM start from a simple template (SMA, momentum) and refine along that vein for many iterations without exploring orthogonal signal families. The local-minimum is the LLM's prior, not the data. **Mechanistic confirmation:** Simhi et al. 2026 ("Old Habits Die Hard: How Conversational History Geometrically Traps LLMs", arXiv 2603.03308) measures this directly across 3 model families × 6 datasets — once a phenomenon appears in a turn, it persists, with Spearman 0.78 between probabilistic (Markov trace) and geometric (hidden-state angle) measures. **Mitigation hint:** the paper shows the trap *dissolves under topical inconsistency* — randomly shuffling unrelated prompts breaks carryover. Practical implication for strategy ideation: deliberately interleave unrelated topics in any LLM-driven search loop, don't let it stay on one thread for many iterations. See `methodology/multi-objective-search.md` for the structural escape route (move to bounded primitive grammar + Pareto search).
- **Single-objective optimisation overfits at any scale.** Feishu IS-parameter-space exhaustion was reached at modest sweep size; further tuning was flagged as overfitting risk. Backtesting's thin samples (N=6, N=32) make any single-metric ranking noise-dominated.
- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (López de Prado).** Expected max Sharpe under the null grows with √log(N_configs). Any sweep across more than ~10 configs needs DSR or PBO as a gate, otherwise the winner is structurally noise. Pre-register the gate before running the sweep.

## Data sourcing (cross-project)

- **`ccxt` is the default multi-exchange OHLCV/funding library.** Wraps ~100+ exchanges (Binance, Bybit, OKX, Coinbase, Kraken, Hyperliquid, etc.) with a unified API. Free, no auth for public endpoints. Supports OHLCV, funding rates, order book snapshots, trades. Use this before reaching for anything custom unless an exchange explicitly disables it (Hyperliquid OHLCV via Freqtrade is one such case — see `backtesting/wiki/decisions/002`).
- **Free historical funding rate JSON** is published by Binance and Bybit going back years, no auth. Cheapest path to test the CEX→DEX funding lead-lag hypothesis (`backtesting/wiki/learnings.md` open #6).
- **CEX OHLCV as proxy for pre-Hyperliquid history.** Binance BTC perps go back to ~2019; Hyperliquid only ~2023. Use CEX history to *train* regime detectors and HMMs across more cycles. Do **not** use CEX history to *evaluate* a Hyperliquid strategy — microstructure (fee, funding mechanism, liquidity, slippage) differs.
- **`yfinance`** — fine for daily/hourly equities + ETFs + spot crypto (BTC-USD). Useless for perps, funding, intraday <1h, or anything Hyperliquid-specific. Reach for it only when you want equity/macro covariates (SPY, DXY, VIX) as regime inputs to a crypto model.
- **`Scrapling`** (and other scrapers) — wrong tool for market data. Bookmark for non-API sources only (CryptoQuant dashboards, Coinglass heatmaps, on-chain UI-only metrics). Default to ccxt + direct exchange APIs first.
- **Hyperliquid S3 L2 book snapshots** (`s3://hyperliquid-archive/market_data/`) — free, public, massive. Only worth ingesting when the SaR slippage proxy hypothesis (`backtesting/wiki/learnings.md` open #8) becomes the active experiment.

## Open hypotheses (cross-project)

1. **Multi-objective Pareto search beats single-metric max** when objectives are negatively correlated under noise (Calmar-bull, Calmar-bear, ulcer, turnover-adjusted PF, OOS-stability). Inspired by `references/divergence_portfolio_theory.md` — same logic as α-portfolio for distribution fitting.
   - **Test:** scaffold an NSGA-II run on a small primitive grammar (~6 signal families × 2–3 hyperparams) once a subproject has enough independent trades per fold.
   - **Open methodology:** see `methodology/multi-objective-search.md`.

2. **Strategy retirement / kill criteria are missing from both subprojects.** Per Dan Davies (ex-quant, in the comment thread of Leek 2025): *"quant funds survive in the long term because of fundamentally non-quantitative attributes of their managers; it is a very rare person indeed [who] combines the common sense to turn the model off when it is breaking down, with self-discipline to resist the temptation to tinker."* Neither feishu's `trend_vol_v4` nor backtesting's `SmaRegime180` has a documented condition under which it would be retired. This is the real survival mechanism — risk *management*, not signal *quality*.
   - **Test:** before any strategy graduates from leaderboard to paper-trading, write a retirement rule of the form: "kill if rolling-90d Calmar drops below X, or if drawdown exceeds Y% of historical max DD, or if regime indicator Z flips for N consecutive bars."
   - **Open methodology:** add `methodology/kill-criteria.md` once the first concrete rule is drafted (don't write it speculatively — wait for the first real candidate).
   - **Supporting evidence (2026-05-09 feishu pull):** Pang & Lin 2025 (5-state correlation regime classifier on Chinese A-shares) + Ravagnani et al. 2026 (`h_robust = h_standard × (1 − δ/σ²)`, continuous shrinkage replacing binary skip rules) both provide concrete *mechanism templates* for kill-rule construction. Ravagnani in particular suggests the right form is **continuous shrinkage of position size** as forecast-error/vol ratio rises, not a binary on/off switch. When the first kill rule is written, prefer continuous shrinkage over hard cutoffs.
   - **First concrete kill rule drafted (2026-05-10):** `backtesting/wiki/decisions/004-kill-criteria-sma-regime-180.md`. Combines hard-kill triggers (MDD > 5.5% = 3× backtest, six straight stops, rolling 365d return ≤ 0 for 30d, walk-forward Calmar < 2) with a Davies–Ravagnani continuous-shrinkage formula that ties live size to rolling 180d PF × Calmar × bull/bear ratio. Pattern is exportable to feishu's `trend_vol_v4` once cross-cycle validation data exists. Promote a generic `methodology/kill-criteria.md` only after the second concrete rule is written.

6. **Cross-cycle CEX validation works as a training-set check (2026-05-10).** SmaRegime180 was tuned on Hyperliquid 2024-26 (one bull + one bear). Backtested unchanged on Binance BTC perp 2019-26 (6.7y, 2 bulls + 2 bears, 92 trades): Calmar 7.23 vs Hyperliquid 8.68; win rate 21.7% vs 21.9%. Strategy generalises. **Methodology pattern:** before promoting any single-venue-tuned strategy to paper-trading, run unchanged on CEX history covering ≥2 regime transitions outside the tuning window. Use CEX history for *generalisation testing*, not for evaluating the venue-specific strategy (microstructure differs — see "Data sourcing" above). Worst-case revealed: 2022-style deep bears flip PF to 0; slope-gate filters cap MDD but opportunity cost is 12 months. Strategy is "ride bulls, stay flat in bears" — exactly what regime filters should do, but the kill criteria need to anticipate this regime as a contained-failure rather than triggering a hard stop.

3. **First real-data Pareto datapoint (2026-05-09, refined 2026-05-10):** `HmmRegime4` backtest result was the first case where two strategies disagreed across the proposed Pareto objectives. **2026-05-10 update:** rolling-window refit (`HmmRegime4Rolling`) shows the look-ahead absorbed ~50% of the alpha. Honest comparison vs SmaRegime180: SmaRegime180 wins on SQN (1.02 vs 0.59), Profit Factor (2.72 vs 1.31), and per-trade economics; HmmRegime4Rolling wins on win rate (37.7% vs 21.9%) and trade density (53 vs 32) — but loses outright on the project's co-primary metric. Multi-asset run on 7 Hyperliquid majors is decisively negative (−5.62%, MDD 14.7%). The Pareto-front frame still applies (the two strategies have different shapes) but the HMM family is now downgraded: it does *not* generalise across coins with shared params, and on BTC it ranks below SmaRegime180 on SQN. Implication: don't sweep over HMM family with a single hyperparameter set — needs per-coin tuning AND DSR gate before claiming signal.
   - **CEX cross-cycle confirmation (2026-05-10):** ran HmmRegime4Rolling unchanged on Binance BTC 2020-26 (267 trades, 4 regimes). Calmar 3.87 vs Hyperliquid 12.11 — Hyperliquid result was inflated ~3× by training-window overlap and the milder 2025 bear. Total return matches Sma exactly (+19.99% vs +20.24%) but via different shape: HMM win rate 39.7% vs Sma 21.7%, HMM MDD 4.35% vs Sma 2.22%. **Decisive finding:** HMM is bear-blind. In 2022 Sma attempted 6 trades (slope-gate flat); HMM attempted 40 with PF 0.60 and 4.02% MDD. In 2025 Sma is +1.61% Calmar +3.59; HMM is −1.24% Calmar −1.56. The "regime-4 bull state" mis-fires exactly when capital preservation matters. **Pareto verdict:** HMM and Sma are non-dominated (HMM wins on win-rate/density/Sharpe/SQN; Sma wins on Calmar/MDD/PF). HMM stays on leaderboard for diversity but is **not a paper-trade candidate** — bear behaviour is structurally weak. See `backtesting/wiki/results/2026-05-10-hmm-regime-4-rolling-cex-validation.md`.
   - **First sweep candidate (revised):** SMA-family is the cleaner baseline. HMM family enters the sweep only with per-coin params or a clear regime-conditional structure (e.g. HMM entry signal *gated* by Sma slope > 0). Funding-rate carry is **ruled out as a standalone signal** — see new entry below.
   - **Bull-window CEX multi-asset confirmation (2026-05-10):** `HmmRegime4Rolling` on 6 Binance majors (BTC/ETH/SOL/DOGE/AVAX/ARB), 2023-01 → 2025-01: **+65.36% return, Calmar 27.73, Sharpe 1.25, MDD 5.69%, all 6 pairs positive** (best SOL +18%, AVAX best-trade +123.69%). The HMM family is **structurally asymmetric, not weak**: −5.62% in bear vs +65.36% in bull is the largest payoff asymmetry in the project. Updates the bear-window downgrade: HMM-multi is a viable **paper-trade candidate when paired with a bear filter** (Sma slope gate is the obvious complement). Pareto frame strengthens — Sma (bear-resilient) and HMM-multi (bull-amplifying) are non-dominated across a much wider performance gap than the BTC-only data showed. See `backtesting/wiki/results/2026-05-10-hmm-regime-4-rolling-multi-cex-bull.md`.

4. **Funding-rate carry is regime-conditional, not broken (2026-05-10 amended).** Original bear-window read (`FundingCarry` on 7 HL majors: −30.16%, win 10.6%, MDD 42.14%) led to "fails catastrophically." **Bull-window CEX run (same strategy unchanged, 6 Binance majors, 2023-01 → 2025-01) flips this:** +12.47% return, **win 52.4%**, MDD 10.65%, Calmar 3.06, 5 of 6 pairs positive. The "negative funding ⇒ short crowdedness ⇒ mean-revert" thesis (Inan 2025) holds in bull regimes; in bears the same signal becomes trend-continuation and loses every entry on the −10% stop. **Implication:** the signal is tradable *conditional on a bull-trend filter*. Open follow-up: regime-gated `FundingCarry` (slope-up precondition) as a leaderboard candidate. See `backtesting/wiki/results/2026-05-10-funding-carry.md` (bear) and `2026-05-10-funding-carry-cex-bull.md` (bull).

5. **Signal-conjunction time-horizon mismatch is regime-asymmetric, not structural (2026-05-10 amended).** Original bear-window read on `HmmCarry` (HmmRegime4Rolling bull-state ∧ FundingCarry funding-negative): −19.59% on 7 HL majors, BTC win 7.7%, "anti-complementary entries from HMM-lag × funding-lead coinciding at tops." **Bull-window CEX run flips this:** +25.77% return, Calmar 13.68, **win 46.2%**, MDD 4.54%, 158 trades. In bull regimes the HMM lag and funding lead align *constructively* (both eventually call long; lead-lag becomes confirmation, not anti-correlation). The conjunction halves trade count vs HMM-only while raising win rate from 35.2% → 46.2% — the filter is doing the work it was designed to do. **Implication:** the meta-allocator-over-signal-families frame stands; intersection logic is viable in bull regimes. Bear regimes still need explicit time-priority or sign assignment (or a regime gate to exclude bears entirely). See `backtesting/wiki/results/2026-05-10-hmm-carry-conjunction.md` (bear) and `2026-05-10-hmm-carry-cex-bull.md` (bull).
