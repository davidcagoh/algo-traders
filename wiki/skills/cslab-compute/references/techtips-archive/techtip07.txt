In working with others at CS you may want to share files.

Your home directory is protected by default so that it can't be accessed
by anyone else.  But you can change this, and you can change it in
sufficiently small ways as to maintain your privacy for all other files
while sharing files you wish to share.

I've written an introduction to basic unix file permissions at
https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~flaps/fileperms.html

If you want to share files under your home directory with others, you
probably want to change your home directory to be mode 711 (rwx--x--x).
This would mean that other people could specify files under your home
directory if they know their name, but they still couldn't read nor write
your home directory itself.  (However, you still need to use appropriate
file permissions on the files (and directories) themselves; don't assume
that people can't guess file names.)

On the other hand, your directories under /w/20251 and /w/20252 are
world-readable by default (rwxr-xr-x).  So you don't need to open it
up further to grant people access to your files, although you could use
the chmod command to protect it further if desired.

For some files, group access by the group of MScAC students might suffice
(the group is named "pmstudgrp").  But if you need finer access control,
the easiest way to do this is to create new groups.  E-mail me what you
want to do and we'll figure out what group(s) to create.  You can change
the group of a file you own with the "chgrp" command, and you can change
it to any group you are a member of.  The "id" command will show you
which groups you are a member of.

Any questions, please ask!
