Greetings,

The Department of Computer Science runs two separate computing facilities:
one for research computing, one for instructional computing.  The research
facility is called CSLab.  The instructional computing facility is now called
"teaching labs" or "teach.cs" (but was formerly called CDF, in
case you've heard of that).

For research computing, you have created a CSLab account which enables you
to ssh to "cs.toronto.edu".  This is actually a nickname for the computer
"apps0.cs.toronto.edu".  There is also apps1.  These "application servers"
are meant for lightweight computing tasks such as e-mail, editing files,
and small program runs.

There are also "computation servers".  Some of these are quite substantial
machines.  Their specifications are described at
https://support.cs.toronto.edu/systems/linuxcompute.html
Some of them can be logged in to interactively at any time; others are
accessed by submitting jobs via "Slurm".  All is described on that web page.


Please restrict your use of the apps machines to interactive, low-cpu-using
applications.  Computationally-intensive and/or long-running processes
should be run on the comps machines, because they have more CPU power and
because they are used less interactively.


As to the teaching labs:  you might get an account there if you are
taking a course which makes use of that facility.  Many graduate courses
use research computing facilities instead.  But if you are taking a
teach.cs-using course, you will get an account on the teach.cs machines.
The name of the account will be the same as your UTORid, but other than
a coincidence of names the account is separate.

If you are a TA for a teach.cs-using course, you will get a separate TA
account at the teaching labs.  These accounts' names begin with a 't'
and then a digit indicating the year it was created; so your TA account
will probably begin with "t5".

Both student and TA teaching labs passwords can be set or reset via
https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/account


And if you're wondering where UTORids and/or yourname@mail.utoronto.ca fit
into all this, the answer is that they don't.  We've been doing computing
and computer accounts much longer than the central U of T IT folks, so we
mostly continue to do things our own way.  Often our way is more useful,
at least for research computing purposes.

However, SGS might send you e-mail to yourname@mail.utoronto.ca, so you
do want to read that too.  Last I heard, you can forward that to CS via
the exciting URL
https://outlook.office.com/owa/?realm=utoronto.ca&path=/options/forwarding
